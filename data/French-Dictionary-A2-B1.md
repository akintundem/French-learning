# French Dictionary — A2 to B1

Everything in one place: **A2 core vocabulary**, **B1 vocabulary**, **occupations**, and the **supplement** (prepositions, idioms, register). Around 1,400 entries, covering the ~2,500–3,000 word target for B1.

**Two rules throughout:**
1. **Learn nouns with their article** — `une table`, not `table`. Gender is much harder to add later.
2. **Sections A1–A2 and the connectors in B1 matter most.** Function words, verbs and connectors appear in every sentence; themed nouns can wait.

# PART C — OCCUPATIONS

Occupations come up constantly: introductions, describing family, talking about plans.


## C1. The two grammar points that matter

**1. No article after `être`.**
`Je suis médecin.` — NOT `je suis un médecin`.
The article comes back if you add an adjective: `C'est un bon médecin.`

**2. Feminine forms follow patterns.**

| Ending | Feminine | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `-eur` | `-euse` | vendeur → vendeuse |
| `-teur` | `-trice` | acteur → actrice |
| `-ier` | `-ière` | infirmier → infirmière |
| `-ien` | `-ienne` | pharmacien → pharmacienne |
| `-e` | unchanged | un/une journaliste |
| consonant | add `-e` | avocat → avocate |

Some traditionally had no feminine form (`médecin`, `professeur`, `écrivain`); modern usage increasingly adds one (`une médecin`, `une professeure`, `une écrivaine`). Both are accepted.

## D2. Backwards verbs

These work opposite to English. The thing is the subject, the person is the object.

| French | Literally | Means |
|---|---|---|
| Ça me plaît | it pleases me | I like it |
| Ce film m'a plu | the film pleased me | I liked the film |
| Tu me manques | you are missing to me | **I miss you** |
| Il me faut du temps | it is necessary to me | I need time |
| Ça m'intéresse | it interests me | I'm interested |
| Ça m'ennuie | it bores me | I'm bored by it |
| Ça me convient | it suits me | that works for me |
| Il me reste 10 euros | there remains to me | I have 10 euros left |
| Ça m'arrive | it happens to me | it happens to me sometimes |
| Il s'agit de | it is a question of | it's about |

**`Tu me manques` is the one people get backwards constantly.** To say "I miss you," the person you miss is the *subject*.

## D6. Politeness and register

The `tu` / `vous` choice is a real social judgement, not a formality.

| Use **tu** | Use **vous** |
|---|---|
| Friends, family, children | Strangers, older people |
| Colleagues your age | Clients, officials, shopkeepers |
| Fellow students | Anyone in authority |
| Animals | Groups (always, even friends) |

When unsure, **use `vous`** and wait — `on peut se tutoyer ?` is how someone offers the switch.

### Softening what you say
| Blunt | Softer |
|---|---|
| Je veux... | **Je voudrais...** |
| Donnez-moi... | **Pourriez-vous me donner...** |
| Non. | **Je regrette, mais...** |
| C'est faux. | **Je ne suis pas sûr que...** |
| Vous avez tort. | **Je ne vois pas les choses comme ça.** |

| French | English |
|---|---|
| Excusez-moi de vous déranger | Sorry to bother you |
| Ça vous dérange si...? | Do you mind if...? |
| Est-ce que je pourrais...? | Could I possibly...? |
| Si ça ne vous dérange pas | If you don't mind |
| Ce serait possible de...? | Would it be possible to...? |
| Je vous en prie | Please do / You're welcome |
| Avec plaisir | With pleasure |
| Désolé de vous déranger | Sorry to disturb you |
| Bien à vous / Cordialement | Regards (email sign-off) |

**Register warning:** `Quoi ?` is blunt — use `Pardon ?` or `Comment ?`. And `Qu'est-ce que tu veux ?` sounds aggressive; `Qu'est-ce que je peux faire pour vous ?` is the polite form.

# PART E — GRAMMAR THIS VOCABULARY NEEDS

Vocabulary alone won't get you to B1. These are the structures the words above slot into — your classes will cover them, this is the checklist.

- **Present tense** — the big irregulars plus `-er / -ir / -re` patterns.
- **Passé composé** — past tense, with the `avoir` vs `être` split.
- **Imperfect vs passé composé** — background/habit vs completed action. The core of telling any story.
- **Plus-que-parfait** — for events before other past events.
- **Futur proche** (`je vais + inf`) and **future simple**.
- **Conditional** — `je voudrais`, `on pourrait`, `si j'avais... je ferais...`
- **Subjunctive** — after `il faut que`, `bien que`, `pour que`, `avant que`, `je veux que`, and verbs of emotion or doubt.
- **Negation** (`ne... pas`, `ne... jamais`, `ne... rien`, `ne... personne`) and question forms (`est-ce que`, intonation, inversion).
- **Adjective agreement and placement.**
- **Partitive articles** (`du, de la, des`) — trips people up constantly with food.
- **Pronouns** — `le, la, lui, y, en` and relative `qui, que, dont, où`.
- **Passive voice** and **reported speech** (`il a dit que...`).
